This post covers specific historical vocabulary borrowings, migration hypotheses, and a comparison with the Dravidian family.

### 1. Specific Vocabulary Borrowings
Because Austroasiatic (Munda) and Indo-Aryan languages have coexisted for millennia, they heavily influenced each other. Early Indo-Aryan settlers borrowed agricultural, flora, and fauna terms from indigenous Austroasiatic speakers.

#### Agriculture & Objects:
* Lāñgala (Sanskrit for "plow") comes from the Austroasiatic root for plow (compare to Santali nahel / Khmer onkar).
   * Karpāsa (Sanskrit for "cotton") originates from Austroasiatic roots (compare to Santali kaskom).
   * Tāmbūla (Sanskrit for "betel leaf") is borrowed from Austroasiatic languages of Northeast India/Southeast Asia.

#### Flora and Fauna:
* Gaja (Sanskrit for "elephant") and Matanga (elephant) are believed to be Munda loanwords.
   * Kadali (Sanskrit for "banana") is derived from Austroasiatic terms for the plant.
   * Mayūra (Sanskrit for "peacock") is structurally linked to Munda prefixes for birds.

### 2. The Migration & Maritime Hypotheses
The arrival of Austroasiatic languages in India remains a subject of intense genetic and linguistic debate:

* Overland vs. Maritime Routes: While older theories suggested a purely overland migration from Southeast Asia through Northeast India, recent archaeogenetic and linguistic models suggest a more complex route.
* The Neolithic Expansion: The consensus is that male-dominated migrations of Austroasiatic agriculturalists brought Austroasiatic languages into Eastern India roughly 4,000 to 4,500 years ago. They introduced Austroasiatic terms for wet-rice cultivation into the subcontinent.
* Genetic Footprint: Modern Munda speakers carry a distinct East Asian genetic component (specifically via the Y-chromosome haplogroup O1b1a1a-M95) mixed with local South Asian ancestry, proving a clear biological and cultural link to early Southeast Asian populations.

### 3. Comparison with the Dravidian Language Family
To contextualise South Asian linguistics, here is how Austroasiatic stacks up against India's other major indigenous language family, Dravidian:

| Feature | Austroasiatic (Munda Branch) | Dravidian Family |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Location | Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Meghalaya | Southern India (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh) |
| Major Languages | Santali, Mundari, Ho, Khasi | Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam |
| Origin Theory | Migrated from Mainland Southeast Asia (~2500–2000 BCE). | Indigenous to South Asia or linked to the Indus Valley Civilization. |
| Word Formation | Uses Infixes (inserting elements inside a root word) and prefixes. | Strictly Agglutinative (suffixes are glued to the end of a root). |
| Gender System | Divides nouns into Animate vs. Inanimate. | Divides nouns into Rational vs. Irrational (Human vs. Non-human). |
| Influence on Sanskrit | Contributed vocabulary mostly related to farming, nature, and everyday life. | Influenced Sanskrit grammar heavily (e.g., retroflex consonants, gerunds). |
